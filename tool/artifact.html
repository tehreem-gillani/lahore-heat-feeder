When should a Lahore street charge its e-scooters?

Thirty-four Lahore homes, measured every minute from June 2018 to May 2019, put on one neighbourhood transformer. Add a scooter to some of them, pick when they charge, and see how much faster the transformer's insulation ages over a year. In this data the summer load peaks around midnight, so the charging time matters more than the energy.

Four days up close

What is measured

Household load comes from PRECON (LUMS): one-minute readings from 42 Lahore homes. 41 were in the download and 34 have every month at least half measured; those 34 are summed as if they shared one transformer. They are really spread over the city. Gap-filled minutes in the published files are replaced by the same house's mean for that month and hour.

Air temperature is NASA POWER hourly data for Lahore.

What is modelled

Scooter energy is 21.3 Wh/km at the battery (WMTC part 1, UN GTR No. 2 road load) and a charger efficiency of 0.85: a commuter rides 24 km a day, Monday to Saturday, and a delivery rider 96 km every day. The transformer follows the IEC 60076-7 difference equations for a distribution transformer, with air temperature as ambient.

The page stores load as 10-minute means, so peaks read a little lower than in the report, and its ageing results differ from the report by up to 2 percentage points.

Read with care

Houses per transformer, power factor, charger power and riding habits are assumptions. Absolute ageing depends heavily on the rating, so compare runs on the same rating. Peak windows are from NEPRA's 2026 LESCO tariff (clause 10).
